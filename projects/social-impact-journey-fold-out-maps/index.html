A Social Impact Journey — Fold-Out Maps

Designing Pocket-Sized Maps for Urban Exploration

Client
ESCP Business School, Spain
Role
Graphic Design · Information Design · Print Design

01 Project Overview

As part of A Social Impact Journey, a workshop-based learning experience developed for ESCP Business School in Madrid, I designed five foldable neighbourhood maps to support students in exploring the city's cultural and social landscape.

The maps combined local information, visual guidance, and learning activities in a compact, portable format. I developed a consistent visual template that could be adapted to different neighbourhoods.

02 The Challenge

The challenge was to organise dense cultural and social information into a printed map that was engaging, readable, and practical to use.

Beyond the content, the physical format was essential. Students needed a map they could easily carry, unfold, navigate, and put back in their pockets while exploring the city.

The design also needed to work as a reusable system across different neighbourhoods.

03 The Approach

I experimented with different paper sizes and folding techniques, creating several physical prototypes that I tested with student participants.

During the tests, I observed how easily participants could handle and use each format. The students also voted for their preferred option, helping us select the most practical and user-friendly design.

I treated each folded panel as an individual editorial layout while maintaining consistency across the entire map. A blue-and-yellow palette, typography, and illustrated icons helped organise the information and make dense content more approachable.

I also developed a reusable graphic template, adapting the content and visual details for each neighbourhood while maintaining a consistent visual system.

04 The Outcome

The project produced five printed neighbourhood maps, developed from a shared visual template and used during the ESCP Business School learning experience.

User testing and participant voting selected the final folding format, prioritising ease of handling and portability.

The reusable template was also retained for future projects, allowing the design system to be adapted to additional neighbourhoods.
