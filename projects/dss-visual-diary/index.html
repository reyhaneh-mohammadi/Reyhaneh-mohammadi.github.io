DSS Visual Diary

20 Weeks, 20 Stories: Bringing the Digital Society School to Life

Client
Digital Society School (DSS)
Role
Visual Storytelling · Editorial Illustration · Content Strategy

01 Project Overview

DSS Visual Diary is a 20-week visual storytelling series created for the Digital Society School (DSS), a collaborative learning environment bringing together creative professionals from diverse backgrounds.

I led the creative direction, content development, illustration, and publication of weekly LinkedIn stories. Through character-driven narratives and conceptual illustrations, the series offered an engaging, human-centred look into the people, projects, and collaborative experiences that shape DSS.

02 The Challenge

Before joining the Digital Society School, I found it difficult to understand what the programme actually felt like from a participant's perspective. I saw an opportunity to make DSS more visible and relatable through authentic, behind-the-scenes storytelling.

The challenge was to turn a fast-paced, 20-week programme into a series of engaging visual stories, highlighting a different side of DSS each week while connecting with future participants, creative professionals, and potential partners.

03 The Approach

Throughout the 20-week programme, I collected moments, conversations, and experiences, selecting the most compelling story to share at the end of each week.

I developed a consistent visual language by combining my minimal, conceptual illustration style with DSS's brand identity and colour palette. Team members, DSS staff, and project stakeholders became illustrated characters, bringing the human side of collaboration into the spotlight.

Each LinkedIn post paired a concise, conversational narrative with an illustration designed to communicate the story at a glance, keeping the content approachable, engaging, and easy to follow.

04 The Outcome

The series resulted in 20 weekly LinkedIn posts, combining visual storytelling with concise, engaging narratives.

Impressions increased from 622 on the first post to 2,455 on the final post, nearly quadrupling across the series.

The illustrations also gained recognition beyond social media, leading to requests for reuse and plans for publication on the DSS website and in the DSS Legacy Book.
